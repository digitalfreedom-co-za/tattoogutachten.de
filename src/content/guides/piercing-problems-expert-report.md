## Which problems can a report clarify?

A report clarifies professional questions about the piercer's work. With piercings, too, questions arise about proper execution, placement, choice of material or aftercare. An expert assesses possible errors in execution, the documented working procedures and the professional standards of the piercing trade.

## What is examined with a piercing?

Depending on the question, this includes:

- proper execution and placement
- the choice of material
- the documented working procedures
- aftercare and the care instructions

If there are doubts about hygiene, the procedures in the studio can also form part of the assessment. More in the guide [Hygiene in the tattoo studio](/guides/hygiene-in-the-tattoo-studio/).

## When is a doctor needed?

For pain, inflammation or other health problems. Where medical findings or harm to health are involved, a medical assessment may be required. The medical diagnosis remains a matter for doctors. An expert documents visible changes and assesses them from a craft point of view; medical records can be taken into account in the assessment.

## What should I keep?

Everything that shows what happened: the invoice or receipt, the studio's care instructions, messages with the studio and photos showing the position and surrounding area of the piercing, ideally also from the first days after it was done. Tips for useful pictures are in the guide [Photographing a tattoo for assessment](/guides/photographing-a-tattoo-for-assessment/); they apply to piercings just the same.

## Can studios commission a piercing report too?

Yes. Tattoo and piercing studios can also commission an assessment, for example when they are accused of errors in execution or aftercare. The assessment does not depend on who commissions it.
