## Is my tattoo really botched?

Only an expert examination can say for certain. A tattoo you do not like is not automatically defective in its workmanship. The assessment distinguishes between personal taste and defects in execution that can be established objectively.

Signs of a possible defect include uneven lines and contours, patchy shading and colour areas, uneven distribution of ink in the skin, scarring, or clear departures from the design that was agreed. More on this in the guide [Defect or matter of taste?](/guides/defect-or-matter-of-taste/).

## Why should I record the condition straight away?

Because some questions become hard to answer later. The more time passes after the tattoo and the more it changes afterwards, the harder it is to assess individual points.

Take clear photos as early as possible and keep the pictures from the session and the healing phase as well. The guide [Photographing a tattoo for assessment](/guides/photographing-a-tattoo-for-assessment/) explains how.

If you are thinking about having the tattoo touched up, reworked or removed, have an expert document its condition first. Once that work is done, the original condition can no longer be seen.

## Which documents should I keep?

Keep everything that shows what was agreed and what happened. Particularly useful are:

- the agreed design or sketch
- the invoice, receipt or agreement with the studio
- the aftercare instructions you were given
- messages and emails with the studio
- photos from the session and the healing phase
- medical records, if you received treatment

Which documents are actually needed in your case is clarified in the [free first consultation](/booking/). What happens after that is set out under [Process and fees](/process-and-fees/).

## What if the skin changes or hurts?

If you have health problems, see a doctor. An expert can document visible changes and assess them from a craft point of view. The medical diagnosis, the finding of any harm to health and the medical cause, however, remain matters for doctors.

## How does an expert report help?

A [tattoo report](/services/tattoo-reports/) establishes what the workmanship shows and explains its findings in a way others can follow. It can help you handle a dispute with the studio on a factual basis and provide grounds for an out-of-court settlement. More on the page [Support in disputes](/services/disputes/).

Whether you are entitled to damages or compensation for pain and suffering is a legal question. It is answered by a lawyer, not by the expert.
