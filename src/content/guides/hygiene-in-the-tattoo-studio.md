## Why does hygiene matter so much in tattooing?

Tattooing and piercing break the skin. Hygiene requirements therefore play a decisive role in the tattoo and piercing trade, for clients and studios alike.

## What matters in the studio?

An expert assessment of hygiene looks in particular at:

- how the workstation is prepared
- how instruments and consumables are handled
- skin preparation and disinfection
- compliance with professional hygiene standards
- wound care and aftercare
- the studio's hygiene records and working instructions

## Why do the records count as well?

Because procedures can often only be assessed after the event. Documented working instructions and hygiene records show how the studio works. A report examines whether these procedures meet recognised professional requirements. Where necessary, this includes an inspection of the premises.

## What role does aftercare play?

Aftercare is part of the process. The aftercare instructions the client received from the studio are therefore an important document if questions arise later. Clients should keep them, and studios should be able to show which advice they gave.

## Who commissions a hygiene report?

Clients and studios alike. A client may have doubts about the procedures in the studio after a tattoo or piercing, or a studio may be accused of shortcomings in hygiene and working practice. Lawyers and courts also commission reports when hygiene is part of proceedings. The assessment does not depend on who commissions it. More on the page [Hygiene reports](/services/hygiene-reports/) and under [Expert reports for tattoo and piercing studios](/for-tattoo-studios/).

## Can a report establish an infection?

No. Whether there is an infection or harm to health is for a doctor to establish. The hygiene report assesses the procedures in the studio. If you have health problems, see a doctor first; medical records can be taken into account in the assessment later. Further answers are in the [FAQ](/faq/#assessment).
